Atlanta’s tree canopy

This map uses Georgia Tech’s Atlanta tree-canopy data. The original raster records canopy at a 1 m resolution.

We’ve grouped it into 433 cells from a 1 km grid to make the city easier to explore. Cells at the edge are clipped to the city boundary.

Seeing the spatial pattern

A city can look green overall while some places have much less canopy than others.

The grid keeps those places on the map, so we can see where coverage is concentrated and where the pattern changes.

How different are two places?

Choose two cells on the map to compare their canopy coverage.

The colors show the broader pattern; the percentages show how far apart your two selected places are.

Data notes & methodology

This map displays 433 supplied polygons. The original grid spacing is 1,000 m in the source projected coordinates; displayed polygons are clipped to the city boundary. A grid cell is not a census tract or a named neighborhood.

Calculation basis requires confirmation. The team reports a full 1 km² denominator. However, the supplied canopy_pct and canopy_pct_final fields differ: grid 16 reports 0.456% and 53.83%, respectively. This version displays the final field without recalculation. Do not interpret it as a verified whole-cell or clipped-area measure until the original calculation is confirmed.

Five cells report 0% while canopymean is null: 323, 324, 350, 351, 586. They retain their reported values and carry hatching. Zero canopy and unavailable raster data have not yet been distinguished. Comparisons involving these cells carry a verification note.

Source attribution was supplied by the team: Urban Tree Canopy for the City of Atlanta, Georgia Tech Center for Urban Resilience and Analytics (CURA); original raster resolution 1 m. The 1 km grid aggregation is the team’s processing, not the original raster resolution. Display field: canopy_pct_final. Values are displayed to one decimal place; differences use original precision. Grid IDs and row/column indices come directly from the team export. Coverage is not a direct measure of shade experienced by residents.
